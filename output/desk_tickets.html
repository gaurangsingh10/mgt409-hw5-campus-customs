This is the one I was most nervous about, because there's a trap in it: the shirt is out of stock, and the obvious move is "order more." The Quartermaster didn't fall for it. It called get_invoice(501), saw that the overdue $840 bill is the rush reprint of the exact tee and size, and said in its summary "I did not file a duplicate restock" Ticket 101 → delegations. That's the judgment I'd want from a real employee. If it had filed another $8 × N restock, we'd have paid twice for the same shirts. The Treasury Droid filed exactly $840 (the MCP tool would have rejected anything else), and the Protocol Droid's reply offered M/L/XL with real counts and called Sep 5 an estimate. Why not a straight A: it took 14 model calls and 40,017 tokens to answer "do we have a small tee?", and the boss alone used 19,078 of those. The Quartermaster also made three lookups it didn't need (get_shop_date, list_vendors, list_approval_requests). For a $28 sale that's a lot of machinery. It's worth it here only because the ticket was secretly about an overdue bill.

Cleanest run of the day. The boss sent it to the Hangar Chief and nobody else Ticket 102 → actual. Ticket 103: B+

All the numbers were right: 8 of 20, 12 short, 15% off $58 = $49.30, $986 total, 12 × $22 = $264, vendor 1 with 5 lead days → ~Sep 5. The boss's final summary even said the $264 restock "would exceed the shop's $160 checking balance", which is real situational awareness, since this run happened after I'd paid 101 and 102.

What dragged it down: the team handed me three proposals that depended on each other without saying so clearly. The customer reply told the AI Club "a restock proposal is pending", while the boss already knew that restock couldn't be paid for. So when I rejected the restock (the board wouldn't even let me approve it: −$104), the reply became wrong and I had to reject it too Ticket 103 → human decisions. The pattern across all three: every agent was right about its own slice, and every report raised the $160 squeeze, but no agent owned the cross-ticket decision of what to pay first with $3,400 when $3,504 was requested. 3. What would have been simpler as one agent with tools, and why?

Ticket 102, without question. The real work was get_lease_rent_status(1) + get_cash_position + one submit_approval_request(pay_rent). The Hangar Chief did that in 3 calls and 7,423 tokens. The boss spent 5 calls and 13,242 tokens (64% of the ticket) reading the ticket, routing it, and then re-summarizing what facilities had already said. One "shop agent" with the same MCP tools would have done 102 in about 3–4 calls at roughly a third of the cost, and been just as safe, because the safety lives in the MCP server: the $2,400 must-match check, no write access to payments, the human approval on the board.

Ticket 101, mostly. It needs four lookups and two submissions. Splitting it across four agents meant each one re-read context: the ticket was fetched 2×, the shop date 2×, cash 2×, and the boss used 47.7% of the tokens. Across the whole run the boss alone burned 50,629 of 103,520 tokens (49%) on routing and summarizing.

The cross-ticket cash decision would be much simpler for one agent. One agent looking at all three tickets at once would naturally see "$840 + $2,400 + $264 = $3,504 > $3,400" and propose a payment order. Our team couldn't, because each boss run only sees its own ticket.

What the multi-agent split did buy me (so I'd keep a smaller version of it):

  • Permissions by role. The Protocol Droid physically can't file a payment, and the Treasury Droid can't write to a customer. With one agent, one bad prompt or prompt injection in a ticket note could do both.
  • Focused prompts. The customer reply on 101 is warm and private because that agent's prompt is only about tone and privacy. The accounting prompt is only about money.
  • A readable audit trail. "Treasury Droid filed #1" is easier to review than one agent's 30-step monologue.

If I rebuilt it I'd use a lighter router (or no boss at all for single-domain tickets like rent), keep specialists only where permissions differ, and add one "planner" that sees all open tickets and the cash position together.

5. Three new problems the team could NOT solve, and what I'd build

a) Getting money in: we're at $160

The Cash tab ends at $160, under our own $500 floor Cash → End. Every tool we built moves money out or prices things. I approved the AI Club's $986 deal on 103, but there's no way to bill them, take a deposit, or record that they paid. The restock I had to reject could have been paid for by the club's own deposit.

What I'd build: a sales / receivables table and MCP tools create_customer_invoice, record_payment_received, request_deposit(pct) and project_cash(days) (cash forecast including expected receipts). Then either a Sales & Receivables agent or an expanded Treasury Droid, plus a Planner that looks at all open tickets and proposes "collect 50% deposit from the AI Club ($493) → then the restock is affordable." Refunds and deposits would still need my approval.

b) Stock that actually moves

Nothing in the system changes inventory.qty. When the tee reprint lands around Sep 5, the Quartermaster won't know. Tauhid could be told S is still out after it arrived. On 103 we offered the club "the 8 in stock now", but nothing reserves them, so the next walk-in could buy those hoodies. And an approved restock becomes a payment row with no purchase order or delivery tracked.

What I'd build: purchase_orders + stock_holds tables and tools reserve_stock(ticket, sku, size, qty), receive_shipment(po_id, qty), record_sale(sku, size, qty, price) and list_overdue_deliveries(). Then a Receiving / Fulfillment agent that, on delivery, updates stock, releases holds and reopens the waiting tickets (101 → "your S is in").

c) Following up after a "no"

I rejected the 103 reply with the note "Redraft: … offer the 8 in stock at $49.30." There's no way to act on that. The ticket is already resolved, and our own /run route refuses resolved tickets (409) Ticket 103. And even the replies I approved (Tauhid's, #2) are never actually sent; there's no email tool.
